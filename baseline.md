## Settled — established fact, NOT news

- Keir Starmer resigned as Prime Minister; **Andy Burnham succeeded him** (July
  2026), having returned to Parliament via the Makerfield by-election.
- **Rachel Reeves is no longer Chancellor.** John Healey replaced her.
- **Ed Miliband has left DESNZ** for the Foreign Office; Miatta Fahnbulleh
  replaced him as Energy Secretary.
- **Louise Haigh is First Secretary of State**, running the Cabinet Office as
  Burnham's enforcer, and has launched cross-government taskforces on the cost
  of living, public control and community renewal — with regional mayors
  involved, which is the characteristic Burnham twist.
- "No. 10 North" exists: a Manchester-based centre of government taking some
  growth and delivery functions from the Treasury and departments.
- The department formerly "Business and Trade" is now **Business, Innovation,
  Science and Trade**.
- Burnham has committed to the 2024 manifesto's tax pledges (no rises to income
  tax rates, National Insurance or VAT) and to operating within existing fiscal
  rules. An early VAT cut on energy bills has already been announced.

A podcast or article that reports, explains or reacts to any of the above is
telling the reader something he already knows. It is **not** a signal.

## Genuinely open — still worth flagging

These are unsettled, so developments here ARE news:

- **The autumn Budget** is the dominant live question: how Healey funds the gap,
  whether the manifesto tax pledges hold, and whether the fiscal rules are
  rewritten. Defence spending is the named pressure point, and bond markets are
  reported to be watching UK borrowing plans closely.
- What Burnham's government actually does on energy: whether Clean Power 2030,
  the SSEP, connections reform and allocation-round design survive the change of
  Secretary of State intact, and at what pace.
- Fahnbulleh's own priorities, and whether DESNZ tilts from infrastructure
  delivery toward household bills and cost of living. The new junior briefs —
  Energy Consumers, Reindustrialisation, Local Energy and Jobs — are themselves
  a signal about where the department's weight is going.
- Welfare reform and the social care settlement, both repeatedly trailed and
  unresolved.
- Any further reshuffle, resignation or machinery-of-government change, and how
  far No. 10 North actually takes powers from the Treasury in practice.
