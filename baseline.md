## Settled — established fact, NOT news

### Government and personnel

- Keir Starmer resigned as Prime Minister; **Andy Burnham succeeded him** (July
  2026), having returned to Parliament via the Makerfield by-election.
- **Rachel Reeves is no longer Chancellor.** John Healey replaced her.
- **Ed Miliband has left DESNZ** for the Foreign Office; Miatta Fahnbulleh
  replaced him as Energy Secretary.
- **Louise Haigh is First Secretary of State**, running the Cabinet Office as
  Burnham's enforcer, with cross-government taskforces on the cost of living,
  public control and community renewal — regional mayors involved, which is the
  characteristic Burnham twist.
- "No. 10 North" exists: a Manchester-based centre of government taking some
  growth and delivery functions from the Treasury and departments.
- The department formerly "Business and Trade" is now **Business, Innovation,
  Science and Trade**.

### Labour conference, Liverpool, late September 2026

Burnham's first conference speech as PM. All of the following is announced and
settled; commentary and reaction to it is not a signal.

- **The pensions triple lock is being reformed from 2030.** The government calls
  it an "adjustment": pensions rise by inflation or 2.5% as needed to hold them
  at least 30% above average earnings. Slower growth than the original lock, but
  no pension falls. Explicitly packaged with a **National Care Service**, which
  will *not* cover accommodation costs. Burnham claims the change funds it and
  needs no tax rise; the IFS and IfG both say moving an unfunded £15bn
  commitment from pensions to care releases no new money. Early polling is
  favourable. Opposition attacks (Farage, Badenoch) and the Lib Dem split over
  it are reaction, not news.
- **GB Grid** — a new publicly owned network company, established as **an arm of
  Great British Energy and funded from GBE's existing budget**. It will invest
  alongside the private sector and compete with the regulated monopolies:
  National Grid in England and Wales, SSE and Scottish Power in Scotland.
  Businesses gain the right to build their own connection infrastructure with
  GB Grid co-investing, instead of queuing for a network company. Stated aim:
  bring UK energy costs into line with neighbouring European countries within
  ten years. **This is on his patch — treat detail, delivery and industry
  reaction as live, but not the existence of the body.**
- **Burnham has recommitted to net zero by 2050**, rejecting calls from inside
  his own party to drop the phrase.
- He has **opened the door to closer EU ties, including eventual rejoining**,
  while ruling a referendum "nowhere near". Cabinet is reported as divided.
- A **cross-party commission on electoral reform** is promised. The
  Conservatives have refused to take part.
- He has knocked down the idea of calling an early election.

### Energy and bills

- **VAT came off domestic electricity on 1 October** — worth about £45 a year,
  landing the same day the cap rose by about £60.
- The **October–December price cap is £1,723**, up 4%, driven by the Middle East.
- **Jackdaw and Rosebank are both still undecided.** Fahnbulleh was ready to
  recommend approving Jackdaw; the decision was pulled and is not expected
  before the Holborn & St Pancras by-election. Rosebank sits further back.
  Reporting that a decision is imminent has been wrong repeatedly — treat it as
  speculation until a decision is actually announced.
- **AR7 is done** and was judged a success at 8.4GW; around a dozen UK offshore
  wind projects are in execution, and the first AR7 projects are paying into the
  Industrial Growth Fund.
- **AR8 applications closed on 7 August.** Administrative strike prices are
  frozen at AR7 levels, there is a new "Other Deepwater Offshore Wind" category,
  and offshore wind will have separate clearing prices by connection location.
  The Clean Industry Bonus budget is set at £515m with floating ring-fenced.
- **Government has ruled out zonal pricing.**
- Opposition positioning is established: **Badenoch has dropped the Conservative
  2050 target** in favour of a gas-and-nuclear pitch; **Reform** runs a fairness
  argument rather than a denial one, has taken a **£72m donation**, and has
  pledged to cut £250 a year off bills within 100 days.

A podcast or article that reports, explains or reacts to any of the above is
telling the reader something he already knows. It is **not** a signal.

## Genuinely open — still worth flagging

These are unsettled, so developments here ARE news:

- **The Budget on 28 October** is the dominant live question: how Healey funds
  social care, defence and the rest; whether the manifesto tax pledges hold;
  whether the fiscal rules are rewritten; and what happens to green levies,
  which Fahnbulleh has hinted could be moved again. Gilt markets are reported to
  be watching closely.
- **The Holborn & St Pancras by-election on 8 October** — Starmer's old seat,
  Green leader Zack Polanski against Labour's Sagal Abdi-Wali, with new North Sea
  drilling a live local issue. It is already shaping the Jackdaw timetable.
- **January's price cap.** The Bank of England expects a further **24% rise** in
  the new year. Whether a bills support package arrives at the Budget, and in
  what form, is open.
- **GB Grid in practice**: how it is capitalised from GBE's existing budget, what
  it means for connection queues and for the incumbent network monopolies, and
  whether the ten-year cost-convergence ambition survives contact with Ofgem.
- **The AR8 Contract Budget Notice**, delayed and expected in November, with
  results from late November into February. RenewableUK wants AR7-level
  ambition; Michael Shanks has pushed back towards price discipline.
- **Jackdaw and Rosebank decisions**, and what the shift to near-field tiebacks
  means for new licensing. OEUK is pressing hard on the Energy Profits Levy
  ahead of the Budget; the replacement regime is not due before March 2030.
- **Curtailment and grid costs**: past £1bn in 2026, projected at £6.6–7.8bn a
  year by 2030, with network charges on bills rising from £44 to £104 by 2031.
  Zonal pricing is ruled out, so what replaces it is open.
- Whether Clean Power 2030, the SSEP and connections reform survive the change
  of Secretary of State intact, and at what pace. Chris Stark's line — stop
  selling gigawatts, sell investment and jobs — is now the house framing.
- **The Conservative conference in Birmingham** (early October) and what
  Badenoch's energy offer turns out to be in detail.
- Welfare reform, the prisons early-release scheme, and whether Thames Water
  goes into special administration.
- Any further reshuffle or machinery-of-government change, and how far No. 10
  North actually takes powers from the Treasury in practice.
