# Current landscape — what the reader already knows

The summariser reads this every morning alongside `profile.md`. Its job is to
stop settled facts being reported back as if they were news: once a decision
has been taken, a podcast explaining it is a recap, not a signal.

**What this file is, and is not.** It is working input to a language model, not
organisational analysis, and nothing in it represents a Crown Estate view. It
is written plainly and sometimes bluntly because the model has to be able to
tell the difference between a fact already established and a genuine
development — hedged or diplomatic phrasing makes that judgement worse, and the
briefing then reports last month's news back as though it were new. Political
characterisations here are shorthand for "the reader already knows this",
nothing more.

**Keep this current.** It is the one file in the repo that goes stale on its
own. Re-check it after any reshuffle, Budget, or major machinery-of-government
change. The pipeline warns in the log once it is 45 days old.

## Settled — established fact, NOT news

### Government and personnel

- Keir Starmer resigned as PM; **Andy Burnham succeeded him** in July 2026, via
  the Makerfield by-election. **Rachel Reeves is no longer Chancellor** — John
  Healey replaced her. **Ed Miliband left DESNZ** for the Foreign Office and
  Miatta Fahnbulleh replaced him.
- **Louise Haigh is First Secretary of State**, running the Cabinet Office as
  Burnham's enforcer. "No. 10 North" exists — a Manchester-based centre of
  government. Criticism that it is drifting into a detached think tank is a
  running theme by now, not a new development.
- **Labour held Holborn & St Pancras** on 8 October: Sagal Abdi-Wali 45.2%,
  Green leader Zack Polanski 32.8%, majority 3,364 on 38.8% turnout. The Green
  share rose sharply from 10.4% in 2024.
- Labour conference, late September: the **triple lock reformed from 2030**,
  packaged with a **National Care Service** that will not cover accommodation
  costs; **net zero 2050 recommitted**; a **cross-party commission on electoral
  reform** promised, and refused by the Conservatives.

### The Budget, 28 October — delivered

Healey's first Budget. All of this is announced and settled; reaction to it and
analysis of it is not a signal.

- **£100 one-off payment** to around 20 million households in council tax bands
  A to D, paid directly, from January 2027.
- **Standing charges removed from household gas bills**, with costs shifted to
  the unit rate. Treasury estimate: around £45 a year saved for 60% of
  households.
- **The Gas Shipper Obligation** — the levy funding hydrogen — **moved off
  bills and onto general taxation**.
- **The electricity VAT cut is maintained until July 2027.**
- **The bank surcharge temporarily doubles to 6% for 15 months**, part-funding
  the energy support.
- Tax thresholds stay frozen; the higher 55p mileage allowance becomes
  permanent; the cash ISA cut was reversed.
- **£700m was cut from carbon capture and hydrogen** to fund the defence
  investment plan.

### Energy — decided

- **Jackdaw was approved on 21 October** and gas has begun flowing. An **Energy
  Wealth Fund with £2bn of initial capital** was announced alongside it.
- **AR8 parameters are set**: fixed-bottom offshore wind capped at £113/MWh,
  unchanged from AR7; a separate "Other Deepwater Offshore Wind" category;
  Clean Industry Bonus budget £515m with floating ring-fenced. Budget-revision
  powers after bid submission now extend to solar, onshore **and floating**.
- **GB Grid** was announced at conference as an arm of Great British Energy,
  funded with roughly £4bn from GBE's existing budget, to compete with National
  Grid, SSE and Scottish Power.
- **£1bn of National Wealth Fund co-investment** into RWE's 2.8GW **Norfolk
  Vanguard West and East**, alongside around £11bn of private financing.
- **A £9bn bilateral agreement with Japan**, signed by Michael Shanks, securing
  equity and technical backing for 5.9GW of UK floating offshore wind.
- **Zonal pricing is ruled out.** The **SSEP** is delayed to Autumn 2027, with
  pathway options already with the Secretary of State. **TNUoS reform is
  deferred to 2029.**
- Opposition positioning is established: **Badenoch has dropped the
  Conservative 2050 target**; **Reform** runs a fairness argument rather than a
  denial one, with a £72m donation behind it.

A podcast or article that reports, explains or reacts to any of the above is
telling the reader something he already knows. It is **not** a signal.

## Genuinely open — still worth flagging

- **Rosebank is the live decision and the biggest open question on his patch.**
  Fahnbulleh is reported as minded to reject; officials put its emissions at
  roughly seven times Jackdaw's. The parliamentary party is split and the
  numbers are contested — reporting variously describes around 100 backbenchers
  organising *against* approval, and a comparable bloc arguing *for* it. GMB
  and Unite are firmly in favour. Any firming of this, in either direction, is
  news.
- **AR8 is live.** The Contract Budget Notice is due **19 November**; the sealed
  bid window may run 3–9 December; results are indicated for mid-February 2027.
  Around 16GW of offshore wind is eligible, against 8.4GW awarded in AR7. How
  much budget ministers allocate, and whether floating gets meaningful support
  given expectations of continued strict price caps, are both open.
- **The 10-year plan** is confirmed for publication before Christmas.
- **Civil service reform**: the Cabinet Secretary has hinted her review could
  propose restructuring or breaking up departments. Any machinery change
  touching DESNZ matters directly.
- **Welfare**: the Milburn report and its treatment of under-25s on PIP and
  Universal Credit is the next big internal fight.
- **EU relations are moving but unresolved.** Burnham used his Mansion House
  speech to slow closer integration; the UK has begun negotiating payments into
  EU cohesion funds; aligning the ZEV mandate with EU rules is under
  consideration.
- **Supply chain health**: the NSTA reports 40% of offshore supply chain
  invoices are paid late and is using its stewardship powers. Developing.
- Whether Clean Power 2030 survives in practice. Fahnbulleh has pointedly
  declined to recommit to the 95% figure, and independent forecasters expect a
  substantial miss.
